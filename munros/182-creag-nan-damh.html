Munro Kintail

Creag nan Dàmh

917m / 3009ft Grid ref NG983112
Location
Did You Know

Creag nan Damh's Gaelic name means rocky hill of the stag. It is the most westerly and lowest of the seven Munros on the South Glen Shiel Ridge, one of the finest ridge walks in the Highlands, where all seven summits can be climbed in a single day with minimal descent between them.

Field Notes

The approach

Creag nan Damh sits at the western end of the South Glen Shiel Ridge, the eight and a half mile chain of seven Munros running from Loch Cluanie in the east to the edge of Glen Shiel forest in the west. Approached from the west, it's usually the last summit reached on a full traverse of the ridge, or the first if walking it in the other direction.

Character of the route

At only a little over the 3,000ft threshold, it's a modest peak compared with its neighbours along the ridge, a rocky, craggy top with steep but mostly grassy slopes falling south towards Glen Quoich.

Practical notes

Most walkers take it in as part of the full seven-Munro traverse rather than as a standalone outing, since the surrounding terrain offers little reason to visit on its own. The reward for reaching it is the westward view, opening out towards the Forcan Ridge, Knoydart and, on a clear day, Skye.
