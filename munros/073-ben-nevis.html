Munro Fort William

Ben Nevis

1345m / 4411ft Grid ref NN166712
Location
Did You Know

Ben Nevis is the highest mountain in the British Isles and attracts around 125,000 ascents each year. It was once a massive active volcano that collapsed inwards millions of years ago. The north face contains some of the most challenging winter climbing routes in Britain, including the famous Tower Ridge.

Field Notes

The approach

Most walkers reach the summit via the Mountain Track, a broad path built in 1883 to service the old summit observatory. It climbs steeply in switchbacks from Glen Nevis up to the saddle at Lochan Meall an t-Suidhe, then continues in longer zig-zags up the stony west flank to the top, a round trip of around 16km.

Character of the route

The going underfoot deteriorates the higher you climb, from a made path near the start to loose rock and rubble on the upper slopes. The summit itself opens onto a broad, stony plateau of around 40 hectares, easy walking in clear weather but notoriously disorientating in cloud, with steep cliffs close to the path on the north side.

Practical notes

Ben Nevis is Scotland's busiest hill, with well over 100,000 ascents a year, and conditions can shift quickly even in summer. Snow can linger on the plateau into the warmer months, and the exposed upper section is best avoided in poor visibility unless you're confident navigating without landmarks.
